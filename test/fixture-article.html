Council approves new transit funding package after long debate

By A. Reporter · 2 hours ago

The council voted seven to four on Tuesday to approve a transit funding package that adds two new bus routes and extends evening service on the east line. Officials said the change is expected to reduce average commute times by roughly eleven minutes for riders in the eastern districts, where demand has grown fastest over the past five years.

Funding for the package comes from a combination of the existing regional transit levy and a new allocation from the capital improvement fund. The transit authority expects to publish a detailed implementation timeline within the next sixty days, and says initial service changes could begin as early as the start of the following fiscal year.

Several council members asked for additional ridership data before the vote, arguing that the projections relied on pre-pandemic travel patterns. Those members ultimately voted against the measure, saying they preferred to wait for updated figures.

Neighborhood groups reacted with a mixture of approval and caution. Several residents who spoke at the meeting asked the authority to prioritise accessibility improvements at transfer points, which the current plan defers to a later phase of the broader project.

Opponents of the package, including a taxpayers association, have said they intend to seek a review of the funding mechanism. A petition calling for a public comment period has already gathered several hundred signatures ahead of the next scheduled hearing.
